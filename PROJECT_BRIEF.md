# IntentCart — technical project brief

IntentCart turns reviewed workstation requirements into a complete compatible basket in a declared finite catalog, investigates missing specifications and repairs changes while preserving component slots before minimizing price. Decision validity, bounded optimality, human approval and provider execution are separate claims.

The Missions studio uses fictional products, prices and laptop profiles. A separate curated evidence workspace binds manually reviewed manufacturer fields to exact variants, units, dates and explicit example equipment/price allowances. The workbench cannot create checkout. Source review, inventory and preference controls are human operations outside the model action schema.

The optional model proposes search, inspect, clarify driver permission, recommend or defer actions. Strict schema/state validation and durable pre-dispatch budgets constrain those proposals. The deterministic local policy remains available without provider access. Permanent external-call and model budgets default to zero.

PayPal sandbox checkout requires an owned exact quote, explicit basket approval and separate buyer approval. Capture/refund reconciliation checks identities, reference, currency, amount and provider state; uncertain operations preserve retry identities. This is a single-instance sandbox system without real-money commerce, distributed settlement, inventory or fulfillment.

Recorded October 19 live evidence comprises seventeen Haiku 4.5 requests including failures; four supported native-fixture trials and a harder fixture with one supported, one failed and two unattempted trials. One fictional USD 279 basket was captured and fully refunded in sandbox. These are project-reported integration diagnostics, not model superiority, production adoption or a human study. Synthetic planning, matrix, acquisition and recovery studies keep their own denominators and negative findings.
