# IntentCart interface — Outcome studio

The main application at `/` and `/missions` is the Outcome studio, refined on October 12, 2026. **Describe → Explore → Review** organizes the editable brief, procedural 3D workstation and exact sandbox quote. A single decision bar pairs the current result and total with its next action. **Setup / Evidence** connects the hardware view to four separate decision claims; deeper information remains available through **Plan details**. This refines the earlier minimal spatial studio while preserving the Missions planner and its approval boundaries. The interface does not add live model inference or real-product compatibility data. The preference-learning interface at `/learn` remains available and is described separately below.

## Current visual language

Obsidian and slate surfaces frame silver and titanium hardware on a grounded machined platform. Ice cyan marks actions, selection and the current evidence view; restrained amber accompanies changed requirements, unresolved evidence and review states. Text labels carry the meaning alongside color. The palette and engineering details connect the cinematic presentation to assembling a compatible workstation and checking its evidence. Explore gives the 3D workstation the full studio width, with a short heading, an Edit requirements action and a compact decision bar below it. The editable brief occupies the left side only during Describe on wider layouts. Expanding the scene hides that form and the studio heading without changing the fields or plan. Component cards, compatibility evidence and activity sit inside the initially closed Plan details disclosure. Local system typography, dimensional materials, restrained edge lighting and a short entrance animation establish the cinematic presentation. Receipts, engineering evidence and the preference lab are grouped under More options. It is agent-assisted implementation; no human-authorship claim is made.

`spatial-scene.js` uses a locally served Three.js module for WebGL rendering. Original procedural geometry includes individual keyboard keys, recessed connector ports, metal fasteners, monitor stands, cables, a power adapter and the beveled platform. Edge highlights, contact shadows and restrained reflections give the conceptual hardware depth. Original contour screen graphics and the reflection environment are generated locally with canvas; the scene needs no remote model, texture, font, inference service or paid visual asset. The renderer caps pixel density at 1.5 and requests a low-power graphics preference; these settings are implementation choices, not a measured performance claim. Six original SVG illustrations use matching titanium surfaces, dark screens, schematic graphics, recessed ports and braided cable detail in component cards and the non-WebGL fallback.

The interface retains a visible fictional-catalog and sandbox boundary; the scene's accessible description identifies conceptual hardware. It is not a photograph, physical simulation or exact model of a purchasable item. When a compiled goal requests one display, the scene centers one monitor and hides the second monitor and its associated cable branch. A two-display goal shows both. During draft edits, the assembled scene stays tied to the compiled goal, while the changed-brief banner identifies the mismatch. Contour graphics, moving cable lights and selection rings communicate interaction; they are not live data, packet measurements or a compatibility proof. In Evidence view, scene lighting follows only the bounded feasibility state supplied by the controller. It cannot establish optimality, consent or payment completion.

## Spatial interaction

- **Setup / Evidence** switches between the physical arrangement and a contextual evidence lens. The lens separates “Does it work?”, “Is it the best choice?”, “May it be purchased?” and “Did it complete?”. Compatibility and optimality labels come from the current mission certificate; exact approval is still required and execution directs the user to the saved receipt. **Inspect the reasoning** opens the existing detailed fixture evidence. Switching views makes no provider request and changes no mission revision.
- With no plan, the evidence state is explicitly conceptual. Missing or conflicting facts remain unresolved. An edited or unreviewed brief invalidates the visible certificate summaries: the hardware remains the previous compiled arrangement, but feasibility and optimality display “Not established” while the request differs from the reviewed compiled plan. Rebuilding, or restoring the reviewed compiled values, clears that mismatch. No stale success state is carried into the new request.
- The primary scene controls are **Take a tour** and **Separate parts / Reassemble**. **View controls** groups Expand / Exit view, Auto orbit, Top view, Reset view, zoom, motion and sound. Dragging orbits the camera. Reset view restores the perspective camera and assembled positions and enables automatic orbit. Expand reallocates space to the scene by hiding the brief and studio heading.
- The tour is five manually advanced camera stops: laptop, dock, displays, cables and power. Each step focuses that component and presents a short caption. Completed-plan captions are distinct from concept-tour requirement descriptions; neither is a model-generated response. There is no automatic step timer. Manual scene interaction stops the tour. Closing it or completing its final step restores the full perspective and returns keyboard focus to Take a tour. Focused views explicitly disable automatic orbit.
- Clicking a 3D component, a labeled component button or a product card opens a contextual component inspector over the scene. Labeled buttons and cards additionally bring the scene into view and move the camera to that component. A compiled plan supplies the fictional product name, quantity, price and role. The inspector is dismissible and remains separate from the tour caption. Selecting concept hardware before a plan exists does not invent a selected specification.
- When the scene has keyboard focus, Left chooses the perspective view, Right a detail view, Up the top view and Down the front view. Plus and minus adjust zoom. Component buttons provide an ordinary keyboard path to selection.
- **Motion off** pauses scene animation and ambient CSS motion. Reduced-motion preferences keep the scene still; explicit view controls remain usable without animated interpolation. Rendering stops while the document is hidden or the scene is outside the observed viewport. Page lifecycle handlers dispose graphics resources and initialize the scene again after a persisted page restore.
- Sound starts **off**. Explicitly enabling it permits short, locally synthesized interface tones; no audio file or external sound service is loaded.

These controls change local presentation only. They do not revise goals, admit evidence, create quotes, approve a basket, execute payments or call a sponsor API. If graphics initialization fails or the WebGL context is lost, the interface exposes the SVG illustration, reports that 3D is unavailable, disables unavailable camera controls and removes the inactive stage from keyboard focus. The ordinary planning, component and quote controls remain separate from the renderer. These are implemented fallback paths, not a claim of verified behavior on every browser or device.

## Brief, plan and review

**Describe** presents the request, two example setups and a compact requirements digest. **Review & edit settings** opens the fictional laptop profile, exact dollar budget input with a companion slider, display count, resolution and monitor reuse. Refresh rate and driver permission sit under advanced requirements. Changing the trimmed request text disables Build and fresh-plan creation until **Review my request** extracts suggestions. The review action opens the settings disclosure for correction before the separate Build action. Example presets load explicit settings and open them for review. This is a text-review gate, not a claim that the parser fully understands the request or that the user has read every field: reviewed typed fields remain the solver's authority. Invalid form controls reveal their enclosing disclosures and return the user to Describe so validation can focus the field.

Interpretation and structured proposal import open the requirements disclosure and move focus to the laptop field after the busy controls re-enable. Structured model proposals reset driver permission through server validation. An opt-in Astropods button is only added when the server reports the model adapter enabled; the delivered zero-call configuration does not execute inference.

**Explore** hides the brief and presents the full-width scene with one state-driven decision bar. `getGuideState` chooses concise copy and one primary action from actual loading, unreviewed-request, new-plan, changed-draft, missing-evidence, conflict, price-opportunity or ready states. Complete plans show the actual total and budget headroom beside the action. A dirty plan's total is labeled as previous state. The bar can direct a user to request review, update the setup, inspect local specifications, resolve a conflict or open the exact quote. The same bar and action mapping serve desktop and mobile, including states without a payable plan. These are user-invoked actions through the existing handlers; the guidance is deterministic interface copy, not an AI response, chain of thought, new planner or autonomous tool loop.

Changing any of the seven Goal fields or the trimmed outcome text marks an existing plan as a draft mismatch. A visible banner asks the user to update the setup, any open quote is cleared, and quote review, evidence admission and availability changes are disabled while the draft differs from the compiled brief. Updating the plan or restoring the compiled values clears that mismatch. `missions-state.js` compares typed field values independently of property order. These browser guards supplement the server's revision, ownership, source-identity and exact-basket checks; they do not authorize payments themselves.

**Plan details** reveals the connection path, passed-check count, display/reuse settings and an optional lower-price check. The result's three keyboard-operable tabs remain inside this disclosure; conflict navigation and focused evidence navigation open it before moving focus:

- **Your setup** shows component cards and a useful next evidence check. An infeasible result shows the unresolved requirements and available explicit relaxations; it has no payable basket. Total and quote review remain in the shared decision bar instead of a second checkout panel.
- **Why it works** separates checked compatibility constraints, missing fixture specifications, rejected alternatives and source excerpts. “Check potential savings” admits local fictional evidence and recomputes the plan; possible savings are not guaranteed. Conflict explanations apply to the finite catalog and do not claim a minimum-cardinality proof.
- **Activity** lists the stored plan revisions and their actual change details. It does not invent a timestamp chronology or model reasoning transcript.

“Try a change” reveals explicit simulated-unavailability actions on components. The resulting repair minimizes changed component slots before price. Retained markers compare product ID and quantity against the prior plan; the banner reports how many currently selected slots match, including cases where a no-longer-needed monitor is removed. Price changes remain visible. If no complete replacement fits, the interface says so and leaves quote review disabled.

**Review** opens a named native quote dialog only when a complete current plan is eligible. It shows exact component quantities, USD total, expiry and sandbox disclosure. Opening or closing it does not approve a purchase; closing returns the workflow to Explore. The separate approval checkbox precedes loading the PayPal sandbox SDK, and buyer approval remains a separate step. Receipts, specification evidence, engineering evaluation and real retailer discovery also have explicitly named dialogs. Real Channel3 listings remain separate from the fictional compatibility catalog; discovery is disabled with zero authorized calls. No goods, fulfillment or live compatibility certification are promised.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

The interface includes a skip link, labeled controls, visible focus outlines, native dialog behavior, live status messages and an `aria-busy` workspace state. The current workflow button exposes `aria-current="step"`. Result tabs support arrow keys, Home and End, with a single tab stop for the selected tab. Building or updating moves to Explore, focuses the result heading and scrolls it into view on narrow layouts. Entering Describe closes the contextual inspector and tour. Closing the inspector chooses a visible component control or stage/heading fallback for focus; Escape returns focus to the relevant menu summary when it hides a focused menu item. Spatial controls expose pressed states and announce selection, camera changes and tour captions. The scene's accessible label describes keyboard camera controls; the inactive fallback stage is hidden from assistive technology.

The spatial stylesheet adjusts layouts at 1150, 800, 620 and 360 pixels over the retained base stylesheet. At 620 pixels and below, Describe places the brief before the scene. Outside Describe, the same decision bar becomes a persistent bottom panel with the current state, total when available and next action. Its action can resolve a conflict or review changed settings as well as open an eligible quote. A dirty draft labels the previous total and cannot proceed to quote review. Component buttons sit above the compact scene controls; lower detail panels stack. Touch handling allows vertical page gestures, with horizontal movement used for camera orbit. Reduced-motion preferences disable transitions, entrance animation and smooth scrolling as well as continuous WebGL motion. These are implemented behaviors, not a complete accessibility certification. Current browser width checks, screenshot review and subsequent fixes belong in `submission/VALIDATION.md`.

## Retained legacy interface — Mission Control, 2.1

The following design describes the retained preference-learning lab at `/learn`, not the current Outcome studio landing view. Its historical validation results are scoped to that interface.

The interface makes a consequential decision understandable: teach preferences, inspect the model, rehearse changes, challenge a proposal, inspect frozen evidence, approve an exact quote. The visual direction is a restrained science-fiction instrument panel with readable language, original backpack SVGs, local system typography and no external visual assets or paid services. It is agent-assisted implementation; a human-authorship claim is not made.

### Legacy visual language

Midnight surfaces provide hierarchy. Lime marks the primary action and current front-runner; cyan carries model evidence; violet identifies contributions favoring an alternative; amber identifies blocked proposals or unavailable purchases. Labels and signed values accompany color. Thin rules, generous spacing and compact monospace metadata distinguish evidence from navigation. The constellation's rotating rings are decoration; they do not represent live telemetry. Reduced-motion preferences disable motion and smooth scrolling.

### Legacy data and interpretation

- The preference constellation plots the current seven rounded posterior-mean estimates. Its adaptive outer-ring percentage is explicitly labeled. It is not confidence or satisfaction. The sidebar provides numerical estimates alongside the graphic.
- The decision lens compares the front-runner against a chosen displayed alternative. Each signed contribution is preference weight times the difference in illustrative catalog features. Its sum is an approximate model margin, computed from rounded weights; it can differ slightly from the server's full-precision score. Bars share a scale within each comparison and rescale on selection. Exact all-in price difference is separate: the affordability feature uses item price, while budget checks include shipping. Selecting an alternative here performs no request or mission mutation.
- The cockpit shows the current server recommendation, total, budget, remaining headroom, top estimated priority and retained comparisons/version. A no-purchase result shows a hold state rather than a fabricated zero-dollar purchase. No live confidence, traffic or performance counters are invented.
- Mission log lanes separately expose ordered comparisons and applied interventions. They do not invent a shared timestamp chronology.

### Legacy interaction and responsive behavior

The command menu opens with its labeled button, Cmd+K or Ctrl+K and closes with Escape. Its four destinations navigate locally; opening it cannot execute a payment, apply a world or stack over an open approval dialog. Choice shortcuts 1/2/T/S require explicit opt-in, ignore editable fields and repeated/modifier keys, and are disabled while busy, outside the learning view or while a dialog is open. Native buttons remain the default path.

Layouts adapt at 1150, 860, 620 and 360 pixels. Mobile comparisons stack for readable specifications. Navigation becomes a two-by-two grid, the cockpit stacks, charts preserve signed labels, and evidence tables scroll inside their container. Controls use visible focus outlines, semantic labels, native dialogs, a skip link and minimum 44-pixel action targets. Browser checks cover 320/390/760-pixel widths; they do not establish a complete accessibility audit or physical-device certification.

### Legacy validation boundary

Six Node unit tests cover contribution arithmetic, reversal/equality, dimension/finite validation, budget edges and adaptive preference scale. Python tests continue to enforce server authority independently. Browser checks verify command navigation, opt-in/field-safe keys, unchanged mission version on lens selection, four learned answers, shipping, blocked tampering, saved fake recovery, absent receipt consent, exact sandbox quote and no-purchase state. No new actual payment or sponsor search is needed to demonstrate this interface.
